\chapter{Discussion}
The strongest evidence in the project is the target-anchored delta formulation on the held-out January period. It improved over persistence for all four horizons, whereas direct level prediction did not. This is a meaningful modeling result, but its use is conditional on a recent and correctly timestamped laboratory value. The target-free virtual sensor solves a different problem and should not be described as a substitute for a future warning.

The later five-cluster Ridge decision prioritizes repeatability across four chronological folds, not the lowest average fold RMSE. That choice is reasonable for a prototype designed to avoid unstable behavior, but it remains a selection from one month. Its statistical clusters cannot be equated with confirmed process regimes until they are checked against operator records such as startup, shutdown, maintenance, cleaning, grade changes and disturbances.

Several limitations constrain interpretation. The dataset spans only January 2026; sensor semantics and units are incomplete for many tags; free acid is configured as manual laboratory data; and no variables are confirmed controllable. Empirical uncertainty and drift flags identify cases requiring caution, but they cannot prove correctness under a new operating distribution. The deployment path was tested in CSV replay, not against a secured plant endpoint. The prototype also does not establish production hardware, long-term retention, access control, backup, or model-governance arrangements.

These limitations explain the architecture choices. Quality shields, chronological buffers, warm-up, status events, heartbeat, reconnection and read-only storage are not optional interface details. They preserve the distinction between an offline model result and a trustworthy advisory service. The correct next industrial step is controlled shadow operation, not automatic action.
