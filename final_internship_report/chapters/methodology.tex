\chapter{Machine Learning Methodology}
\section{Problem formulation}
Let $x_t$ denote the process information available at minute $t$ and $y_t$ the free-acid measurement. Direct forecasting learns $f_h(x_{\leq t})\approx y_{t+h}$ for horizons $h\in\{1,5,10,15\}$ minutes. Because a current observed level is very persistent over short periods, the project also evaluated delta forecasting:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:delta}
\Delta y_{t,h}=y_{t+h}-y_t,\qquad \hat y_{t+h}=y_t+\widehat{\Delta y}_{t,h}.
\end{equation}
Equation~\ref{eq:delta} focuses the model on future movement rather than reproducing a known current level. It is valid only when the laboratory target is available and fresh. Target-free virtual sensing instead learns $g(x_{\leq t})\approx y_t$ without any current or historical target feature.

Performance is reported with MAE and RMSE:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{MAE}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}|y_i-\hat y_i|,\qquad
\mathrm{RMSE}=\sqrt{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(y_i-\hat y_i)^2}.
\end{equation}
RMSE gives additional weight to larger errors, which is useful for identifying unstable periods, while MAE is readily interpretable as average absolute deviation.

\section{Chronological validation}
The baseline split is the first 70\% for training, the next 15\% for validation and the final 15\% for test. Random shuffling was not used because adjacent one-minute samples are dependent and could make near-identical states appear in training and test. Preprocessing, lag selection, clipping limits, correlation filtering, hyperparameters and blend weights were learned without test fitting.

Later stability work used four expanding chronological blocks. For each fold, imputation, scaling, clustering and local experts were fitted on the preceding rows only. This produces a distribution of fold errors rather than a single validation number, allowing mean performance, variability and worst-block behaviour to influence selection.

\section{Models investigated}
Ridge regression was retained as a regularized linear reference for correlated engineered features \cite{hoerl1970ridge}. Extra Trees and Histogram Gradient Boosting were used in the direct benchmark to test nonlinear interactions. The target-free study additionally considered PLS \cite{wold2001pls}, KNN, XGBoost or LightGBM \cite{ke2017lightgbm} where available, and validation-weighted blends. The final Notebook 05 target-free baseline was a 50/50 Ridge-LightGBM blend. Chronological validation follows the need to respect serial dependence in time-series evaluation \cite{bergmeir2018forecasting}.

The robustness notebooks compared global blends, KMeans-partitioned local Ridge experts, a global/local hybrid and JITL variants using PCA or PLS representation with nearest neighbours. KMeans clusters are statistical feature-space groups, not confirmed operating regimes. JITL did not produce a selection improvement over the global blend.
