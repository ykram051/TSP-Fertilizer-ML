\section{Data source and scope}
The notebooks use a confidential historian export named \texttt{tsp\_1min.csv}, absent from the repository. The generated audit artifacts record 44,640 one-minute rows from 1 to 31 January 2026, 21 numeric process measurements and the target, with 308 missing target values. Rows are sorted by the \texttt{Date} timestamp and every split preserves chronology (Table~\ref{tab:splits}).

\section{Variables and measurement semantics}
The 21 inputs fall into flows (acid lines, ground phosphate, slurry, fuel oil, wash liquid, steam), thermal measurements (steam, attack tank, passage tank, sprayer, dryer outlet gas, hot air, brick), pressure and state signals (steam pressure, dryer depression, passage-tank level), and a recycle and production indicator. The variable registry (Appendix~\ref{app:evidence}) assigns each signal a role: \emph{observed state}, \emph{candidate actuator} (a flow, pressure or fuel setting that an operator may influence) or \emph{uncertain role} (the recycle stream). The two slurry volumetric flows are expressed in m\textsuperscript{3}/h. All other signals are modelled on a standardised scale, so that Ridge regression and tree ensembles are insensitive to the unit in which a tag is expressed; derived quantities are used as engineered indicators rather than as strict physical balances.

\subsection{Distribution of the target}
The target is tightly controlled around 11.3 with rare excursions (Table~\ref{tab:target}). The 99\textsuperscript{th} percentile is 12.31, whereas the maximum reaches 52.67, giving a skewness of 12.1 and a kurtosis of 959. The Tukey fences $[Q_1-1.5\,\mathrm{IQR},\,Q_3+1.5\,\mathrm{IQR}]=[10.05,\,12.56]$ flag 352 rows, which occur both as isolated spikes (for instance 52.671 on 18 January, 25.964 on 19 January) and as continuous low-acid windows of 15 to 28 minutes (for example 28 January, 22:18--22:45, with values between 8.66 and 10.00). Because the distribution is heavy-tailed, the evaluation reports both MAE and RMSE and analyses extreme periods separately (Section~\ref{sec:regimes}); no target value is deleted automatically.

\subsection{Relationship with the process variables}
Linear association with the target is moderate and uniformly positive (Table~\ref{tab:corr}): no single signal exceeds a correlation of 0.37. Fuel-oil flow, steam flow, recycle, slurry flow and production load all rise with the free acid, which points to an interaction between feed intensity, heat load and internal circulation rather than to a single dominant driver. This observation motivated feature families that combine sources (ratios, gradients) instead of relying on raw values. Correlation is used for screening only.

Shifting a signal by a few minutes slightly increases its correlation (Table~\ref{tab:corr}): the best lag is 1 to 5 minutes for most flows and 10 minutes for steam pressure and ground-phosphate flow. The gains are small, so no single dead time exists; the model therefore receives several lags and rolling statistics rather than one delayed copy (Section~\ref{sec:features}).

\subsection{Physical plausibility}
Several flow signals take small negative values (475 for steam flow, 451 for fuel oil, 420 for slurry volume, 354 for the production balance), consistent with noise around zero during low-flow or stopped conditions, whereas 4,861 negative values of the dryer depression follow the draft sign convention. A single universal rule would therefore be wrong; the pipeline keeps the values, adds a missingness indicator and relies on percentile clipping of derived features.

\section{Data quality and preprocessing}\label{sec:prep}
The findings of Section~\ref{sec:eda} translate into conservative rules. All fitted preprocessing is learned on the training period only. The first 30 rows are removed because the longest lookback is 30 minutes. Missing cells receive a missingness indicator and a training-median imputation. Derived ratios use the guarded form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ratio}
r(a,b)=\frac{a}{b+\operatorname{sgn}(b)\,\epsilon}\ \text{ if }|b|>\epsilon,\ \text{ otherwise missing},\qquad \epsilon=10^{-6},
\end{equation}
infinities are replaced by missing values, and process-derived features are clipped to the training 0.1\textsuperscript{th} and 99.9\textsuperscript{th} percentiles. Together these rules stop unstable ratios from dominating a model without fitting anything on validation or test data.

\section{Feature engineering}\label{sec:features}
For each source $x$ and time $t$, the accepted temporal features are the current value, selected lags $x_{t-\ell}$, rolling means over $w\in\{10,30\}$ minutes, a 10-minute rolling standard deviation, a five-minute difference $x_t-x_{t-5}$, and the least-squares slope over the last 10 samples,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:slope}
b_t=\frac{\sum_{k=0}^{9}(k-4.5)\,\bigl(x_{t-9+k}-\bar x_{t}^{(10)}\bigr)}{\sum_{k=0}^{9}(k-4.5)^2},
\end{equation}
which is set to missing if the window contains a missing value. Lags were chosen among $\{1,5,10,15,30,60\}$ minutes from training-only correlation, mutual information, stability across chronological blocks and a limited process-response prior, keeping at most three lags per source; 20 of the 21 sources retained lags 1, 5 and 10.

Process-aware features add total acid flow, acid-flow imbalance $|F_{A1}-F_{A2}|$, the acid-to-phosphate ratio, total slurry volumetric flow, a slurry-density proxy $F^{t/h}_{\text{slurry}}/F^{m^3/h}_{\text{slurry}}$, temperature gradients (attack minus passage tank, hot air minus dryer outlet gas, steam minus sprayer slurry), a steam-energy proxy $P_{\text{steam}}T_{\text{steam}}$, a drying-intensity proxy, a recycle-to-production ratio and five 10-minute flow coefficients of variation $\sigma_{10}/(|\mu_{10}|+\epsilon)$. Only the two slurry volumetric flows are confirmed additive; every other relation is a proxy.
